## 题目

Some people think that the detailed description of crimes on newspapers and TV has bad consequences, so this kind of information should be restricted in the media. To what extent do you agree or disagree? Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.

**立场(一句话):** 同意:详细描述伤害受害者、可能被模仿;媒体可以报道必要的信息,但应去掉过度的细节。

## 中文(用来翻译)

如今,媒体可以更自由地详细报道犯罪事件,这引起了很多担忧。有人认为这类信息应该受到限制,我同意这个观点。

有几个原因说明详细的犯罪报道应该受到更严格的控制。受影响最大的是犯罪的受害者。不顾及感受或过于详细的报道会严重侵犯受害者的隐私,给他们和他们的家人带来更多的情感痛苦。此外,公众从了解犯罪的每一个细节中获益很少。血腥的描述会让读者感到不适,也会吓到年轻人。更糟糕的是,潜在的罪犯可能会模仿报道中描述的手段,把详细的报道当成一种指南。例如,一起犯罪的细节被广泛报道后,有时其他地方会出现类似的犯罪。

诚然,报道犯罪也是有用的。有人认为媒体有责任让公众了解犯罪的趋势。的确,媒体通过向人们提供重要的信息,比如新型诈骗手段和自我保护的方法,在公共安全方面发挥着重要作用。然而,做到这一点并不需要涉及过多的细节。如果记者专注于公众需要知道的事实,去掉血腥或敏感的细节,他们就能准确地报道犯罪事件,同时尽可能减少对受害者和公众的伤害。

总之,虽然报纸和电视需要向公众提供关于犯罪的关键信息,但我认为应该限制过度的细节描述,以减少它们的有害影响。

Today, the media are freer to report crimes in great detail, which has raised a great deal of concern. Some people believe that this kind of information should be restricted, and I agree with this view.

There are several reasons why detailed crime reports should be more carefully controlled. The group most affected is the victims of crime. Coverage that is insensitive or overly detailed can seriously invade victims' privacy and cause them and their families further emotional suffering. In addition, the general public gains little from knowing every detail of a crime. Graphic descriptions can make readers uncomfortable and frighten young people. Worse still, potential criminals may copy the methods described, treating detailed reports as a kind of guide. For example, after the details of a crime are widely reported, similar crimes sometimes appear in other places.

Admittedly, reporting on crime can be useful. Some people argue that the media have a duty to keep the public informed about crime trends. Indeed, the media play an important role in public safety by telling people about essential information, such as new types of fraud and ways to protect themselves. However, this can be done without going into excessive detail. If reporters focus on the facts the public needs and leave out graphic or sensitive details, they can cover crime stories accurately while causing as little harm as possible to victims and the public.

In conclusion, although newspapers and television need to inform the public about key information on crime, I believe restrictions should be placed on excessive details in order to reduce their harmful effects.
