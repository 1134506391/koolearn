## 题目

Some people say that the main aim of advertising is to improve the sales of products that people do not really need. To what extent do you agree or disagree? Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.

**立场(一句话):** 不同意:有些广告确实推销不需要的东西,但很多广告推销的是真正需要的东西,还有公益广告。

## 中文(用来翻译)

近年来,广告业发展迅速,广告随处可见,从报纸到地铁车厢里的屏幕。有人认为广告的主要目的是提高人们并不真正需要的产品的销量,但我认为这种观点太片面了。

诚然,有充分的理由相信广告常常说服人们购买不必要的东西。作为一种营销工具,广告提供关于产品的有吸引力的、有时甚至是夸张的信息,目的是吸引顾客、提高利润。大多数生产商把广告看作激发购买欲望的有效方式,所以他们用广告来制造原本不存在的需求。例如,最新款智能手机的广告常常说服人们换掉一部还完全能用的手机。

然而,这种观点把情况过于简单化了。第一,许多广告推销的是人们真正需要的产品和服务,比如食品、药品、保险和教育。在这些情况下,广告告诉消费者有哪些选择,理性的消费者可以利用这些信息比较不同的选择,做出明智的决定。第二,公益广告很容易被忽视,但它和卖东西完全无关。提醒人们不要酒后驾车或者鼓励人们节约用水的宣传,目的是提高公众的意识、传播良好的社会价值观。

总之,虽然有些广告确实会误导消费者购买不必要的产品,但我认为不应该如此负面地看待整个广告行业,因为很多广告提供了关于真实需求和重要社会问题的有用信息。

In recent years, the advertising industry has grown rapidly, and advertisements can be found everywhere, from newspapers to the screens on subway trains. While some people claim that the main aim of advertising is to increase sales of products people do not really need, I think this view is too narrow.

Admittedly, there are good reasons to believe that advertising often persuades people to buy unnecessary things. As a marketing tool, it provides attractive and sometimes exaggerated information about products in order to attract customers and increase profits. Most manufacturers see commercials as an effective way to create a desire to buy, so they use them to create needs that did not exist before. For example, adverts for the latest smartphone often persuade people to replace a phone that still works perfectly well.

However, this idea oversimplifies the situation. Firstly, many advertisements promote products and services that people really need, such as food, medicine, insurance and education. In these cases, advertising tells consumers what is available, and rational consumers can use this information to compare options and make sensible decisions. Secondly, public service advertising, which is easily overlooked, has nothing to do with selling at all. Campaigns that warn against drink-driving or encourage people to save water aim to raise public awareness and promote good social values.

In conclusion, although some advertisements do mislead consumers into buying unnecessary products, I believe that advertising as a whole should not be judged so negatively, because much of it provides useful information about real needs and important social issues.
