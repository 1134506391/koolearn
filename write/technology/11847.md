## 题目

Some people benefit from modern communication technology, but some people still do not have access to it. To what extent do you agree or disagree? Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.

**立场(一句话):** 同意「数字鸿沟存在」,但不同意「这是不可避免、可以接受的」。

## 中文(用来翻译)

> 这一版是对照英文修正过的中文。你第 1 次用的中文里,「分歧」「接入权」「福利」「有意识的」这几个词译得不准,直接导致你写出了 disagree、have right to access 这样的词。下次重译用这一版。

不可否认,现代通讯技术已经改变了许多人的生活,但仍有相当一部分人无法享受这些好处。我同意这种**鸿沟**确实存在,但我坚决不同意「这种情况不可避免或可以接受」的说法。

**能使用这些技术的人**获得的好处是巨大的。智能手机和互联网让远程办公、在线教育、远程医疗,以及与世界各地亲友的即时联系成为可能,这些在一代人之前都是无法想象的。例如,在新冠疫情期间,数百万学生通过网课继续学习,许多员工通过在家办公保住了工作。从这个意义上说,能上网的人在就业、学习和社交方面,比无法上网的人有真正的优势。

然而,许多人被**落下**这一事实不应该被默默接受。在农村和发展中国家,不可靠的基础设施和高昂的费用让数百万人无法获得这些机会,而许多老年人则因为不熟悉技术而望而却步。随着日常生活越来越多地转到网上,从银行到政府服务,这种数字鸿沟可能会成为一种新的社会不平等,让无法上网的人处境更加不利。政府和企业不应把它当作进步不可避免的副产品,而应该投资基础设施、补贴上网费用、提升数字素养,让好处分配得更均衡。

总之,虽然我承认现代通讯技术的使用存在不平等,但我不认为这种不平等应该被简单地接受。通过**刻意**、持续的公共努力,这条鸿沟可以、也必须被缩小。

It is undeniable that modern communication technology has transformed the lives of many people, while a significant proportion of the population remains excluded from these benefits. I agree that such a divide exists, but I firmly disagree with the idea that this situation is either inevitable or acceptable.

The advantages enjoyed by those with access are enormous. Smartphones and the internet enable remote working, online education, telemedicine and instant contact with friends and family across the globe, all of which were unimaginable a generation ago. During the COVID-19 pandemic, for example, millions of students continued their studies through online classes, and many employees kept their jobs by working from home. In this sense, connected individuals gain a genuine edge in employment, learning and social life over those without access.

However, the fact that many others are left behind should not be tolerated passively. In rural areas and developing regions, unreliable infrastructure and prohibitive costs exclude millions from these opportunities, while many elderly people are put off by unfamiliar technology. As daily life increasingly moves online, from banking to government services, this digital divide threatens to become a new form of social inequality that leaves the disconnected even more disadvantaged. Rather than accepting it as an unavoidable by-product of progress, governments and businesses should invest in infrastructure, subsidise access and promote digital literacy so that the benefits can be shared more evenly.

In conclusion, while I acknowledge the reality of unequal access to modern communication technology, I do not believe that this inequality should simply be accepted. With deliberate and sustained public effort, the divide can, and must, be narrowed.
