## 题目

Some people think that it is better to use scientific techniques to grow food, while others think that we should grow food naturally. Discuss both these views and give your own opinion. Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.

**立场(一句话):** 两种方法结合:在严格监管下使用科学技术,同时采用可持续的做法。

## 中文(用来翻译)

近年来,在食物生产中应该使用科学技术还是自然方法,这一争论越来越受到关注。虽然两种方法各有优点,但我认为,在严格监管下把两者谨慎地结合起来,是实现可持续粮食安全的最好办法。

支持在农业中使用科学技术的人认为,现代创新对满足全球粮食需求必不可少。转基因、化肥和其他生物技术明显提高了作物产量。例如,在 20 世纪 60 年代,新的高产小麦和水稻品种帮助印度大幅提高了粮食产量,避免了大范围的饥荒。由于世界人口预计到 2050 年将接近 100 亿,支持者认为科学方法对防止饥饿是必要的。

相比之下,支持自然耕作的人强调它的长期可持续性和健康益处。他们认为,大量使用化学农药和化肥会破坏土壤质量,减少生物多样性。许多注重健康的消费者也更喜欢有机食品,认为它更安全、更有营养。

然而,我认为,两个极端都不是答案。科学技术在受到适当监管、并与可持续的做法相结合时,可以提高粮食产量,而不会造成严重的环境破坏。另一方面,纯自然的方法虽然对环境友好,却可能难以养活数十亿人。因此,混合的方法才是最实际的解决方案,比如精准农业——用传感器和无人机只在需要的地方浇水和施肥。

总之,我们不应把科学耕作和自然耕作看作对立的两面,而应结合它们各自的优势。这种平衡的方法既能保证足够的粮食,又能为后代留下健康的环境。

The debate over whether scientific techniques or natural methods should be used in food production has become increasingly prominent in recent years. While both approaches have their merits, I believe that a careful combination of the two, under strict regulation, offers the best solution for sustainable food security.

Supporters of scientific techniques in agriculture argue that modern innovations are essential for meeting global food demand. Genetic modification, chemical fertilizers and other biotechnology have clearly increased crop yields. In the 1960s, for example, new high-yield varieties of wheat and rice helped India greatly increase its food production and avoid widespread famine. With the world's population expected to reach nearly 10 billion by 2050, supporters argue that scientific methods are necessary to prevent hunger.

In contrast, those who favour natural farming emphasize its long-term sustainability and health benefits. They argue that heavy use of chemical pesticides and fertilizers damages soil quality and reduces biodiversity. Many health-conscious consumers also prefer organic food, believing it to be safer and more nutritious.

In my view, however, neither extreme is the answer. Scientific techniques, when properly regulated and combined with sustainable practices, can increase food production without causing serious environmental damage. On the other hand, purely natural methods, although environmentally friendly, may struggle to feed billions of people. A mixed approach is therefore the most practical solution, such as precision farming, in which sensors and drones are used to apply water and fertilizer only where they are needed.

In conclusion, rather than seeing scientific and natural farming as opposites, we should combine their strengths. This balanced approach can ensure both enough food and a healthy environment for future generations.
