## 题目

People around the world now watch the same films. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this? Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.

**立场(一句话):** 利弊题,不用选边:好处是共同的文化体验和更高的制作水准,坏处是威胁文化多样性和原创性。

## 中文(用来翻译)

得益于全球发行网络和流媒体平台,世界各地的观众越来越多地在看同样的电影。这一趋势既带来了相当大的好处,也有严重的弊端,下面我将依次讨论。

最明显的好处是创造了共同的文化体验。当不同国家的人看同一部大片时,他们就有了共同的话题,这有助于国际交流和相互理解。例如,2019 年《复仇者联盟 4:终局之战》几乎在全球同步上映时,从北京到纽约的影迷都在网上讨论同一个故事。此外,这类电影在全球的成功也提高了制作水准,因为电影人要争夺全世界的观众,所以会在质量上大量投入。从经济角度看,一个真正的全球市场让制片方可以把成本分摊到多得多的观众身上,使那些雄心勃勃、技术要求高的项目在财务上变得可行。

另一方面,电影的同质化也带来了明显的弊端。最重要的是,它威胁到文化多样性:如果本地观众都涌向国际大片,本国电影业就难以生存,独特的故事、语言和传统就有被边缘化的危险。看着同样的全球系列电影长大的孩子,可能对本国的电影知之甚少。人们还担心电影会变得越来越公式化——为了吸引尽可能多的观众而设计,而不是挑战观众或体现某种特定的文化,这会逐渐削弱电影业的原创性。

总之,虽然全球流行同样的电影能带来共同的体验和更高的制作水准,但它也危及文化多样性和创作的原创性。理想的做法是,在享受国际电影的同时,积极支持和保护本土电影业,这样全球化的好处就不会以牺牲文化认同为代价。

Thanks to global distribution networks and streaming platforms, audiences across the world increasingly watch the same films. This trend brings both considerable benefits and serious drawbacks, which I will consider in turn.

The most obvious advantage is the creation of shared cultural experiences. When people in different countries watch the same blockbuster, they gain a common reference point, which facilitates international conversation and mutual understanding. For example, when *Avengers: Endgame* was released almost simultaneously around the world in 2019, fans from Beijing to New York were discussing the same story online. The global success of such films also raises production standards, since filmmakers compete for worldwide audiences and therefore invest heavily in quality. Economically, a genuinely global market allows producers to spread their costs over a much larger audience, making ambitious and technically demanding projects financially viable.

On the other hand, the homogenisation of cinema carries significant disadvantages. Above all, it threatens cultural diversity: if local audiences flock to international productions, domestic film industries struggle to survive, and distinctive stories, languages and traditions risk being marginalised. Children growing up on identical global franchises may know little about the films of their own country. There is also a concern that films become increasingly formulaic, designed to appeal to the largest possible audience rather than to challenge viewers or reflect any particular culture, which gradually erodes originality in the industry.

In conclusion, while the global popularity of the same films fosters shared experiences and higher production values, it also endangers cultural diversity and creative originality. The ideal is to enjoy international cinema while actively supporting and protecting local film industries, so that the benefits of globalisation do not come at the cost of cultural identity.
