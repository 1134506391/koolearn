## 题目

Some people think the increasing business and cultural contact between countries brings many positive effects. Others say it causes the loss of national identities. Discuss both of these views and give your own opinion. Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.

**立场(一句话):** 双方都有道理;我的观点是全球化好处很大,但必须主动保护民族认同。

## 中文(用来翻译)

世界越来越被看作一个地球村,贸易、旅游、电影、音乐和艺术都跨越了国界。支持这一趋势的人认为,国家之间日益增多的商业和文化交流带来了许多好处。然而,批评者警告说,这可能会削弱民族认同和传统。本文将先分析双方观点,再给出我自己的看法。

欢迎国际交流的人强调它在经济和文化上的好处。国际贸易极大地增加了消费者的选择,降低了价格,还让较贫困地区的农民和手工艺人进入海外市场,这有助于减少贫困。此外,文化交流促进了不同民族之间的相互理解和接纳。例如,通过国际节日和学生交换项目,年轻人逐渐形成了一种全球共同体的意识。

另一方面,批评者认为,随着各国采用相似的商业模式和文化产品,独特的民族特色可能会逐渐消失。例如,美国快餐连锁店在全球流行,让许多年轻人更喜欢汉堡和薯条,而不是传统菜肴,于是当地的饮食传统正在衰落。同样,年轻人可能热衷于现代的全球潮流,却对自己的习俗和节日知之甚少,这会让他们逐渐脱离自己的文化根源。

总之,我认为,虽然全球化为相互丰富提供了巨大的机会,但积极保护那些让世界变得多样的民族认同至关重要。例如,学校可以教授传统节日和手工艺,让年轻人在享受全球文化的同时不忘记自己的文化。随着全球一体化加速,在拥抱多样性和保护文化遗产之间找到平衡变得越来越重要。

The world is increasingly seen as a global village, where trade, tourism, films, music and art cross national borders. Supporters of this trend argue that growing business and cultural contact between countries brings many benefits. However, critics warn that it may weaken national identities and traditions. This essay will examine both views before presenting my own.

Those who welcome closer international contact point to its economic and cultural benefits. International trade has greatly expanded consumer choice and lowered prices, and it also gives farmers and craftspeople in poorer regions access to overseas markets, which can help reduce poverty. Moreover, cultural exchange promotes mutual understanding and acceptance among different peoples. Through international festivals and student exchange programmes, for instance, young people develop a sense of global community.

On the other hand, critics argue that as countries adopt similar business practices and cultural products, unique national characteristics may gradually disappear. For example, the global popularity of American fast-food chains has led many young people to prefer hamburgers and fries to traditional dishes, so local food traditions are declining. Similarly, young people may embrace modern global trends while knowing little about their own customs and festivals, which gradually cuts them off from their cultural roots.

In conclusion, I believe that while globalisation offers immense opportunities for mutual enrichment, it is essential to actively preserve the national identities that make our world diverse. For example, schools can teach traditional festivals and crafts, so that young people enjoy global culture without forgetting their own. As global integration accelerates, striking a balance between embracing diversity and safeguarding cultural heritage becomes increasingly important.
